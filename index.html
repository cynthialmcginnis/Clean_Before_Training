Clean Before You Train

You are handed a dataset and asked to train a model on it. Some of the bad data will stop the model. Most of it will not. Your job is to find both kinds before the model makes decisions about real people.

1Pick your dataset

Each dataset has a little over 200 records. Each will train a model that predicts one yes-or-no outcome.

2Train the model on the data as delivered

This is the file exactly as it arrived. Scroll through it, then follow these steps.

  1. Click “Train the model.”
  2. If training stops, read the message and choose a fix.
  3. Click “Train the model again.”
  4. Repeat until the model runs. Expect it to stop more than once.


The file is the data exactly as delivered. It opens in Excel, Google Sheets, or Numbers. Use it to sort, filter, and look for problems. The cleaning itself happens on this page.

4Decide how to clean it

Seven problems are hiding in the two tables above. Each has more than one fix, and each fix costs something. Choose, train again, and compare. Change one thing at a time when you can, so you know what caused the change in the score.

Each time you change a choice, click the button below to train the model again. Nothing is scored until you do.

Your runs

Every run is scored on the same 400 new records. A change of one or two points is noise on a test this size. Look for the moves of five points or more, and look at each group, not only the overall number.

5Choose the run you would hand off

Pick the run whose cleaning choices you can defend. The best score is not always the best choice. You need at least three runs: the data as delivered, plus two of your own. Then create your cleaning log.

The run I am handing off

Next: write your handoff note. Tell the next team what broke, what ran without complaint but was wrong, what you changed and why, what you left alone, and who would have been hurt if this data had shipped dirty. Attach this log.

Every organization and every record on this page is fictional. The data was generated for this exercise and describes no real person or group. The model is a real one, a five-nearest-neighbors classifier, and every score is computed in your browser from your choices. Your work is saved in this browser only.
